Analytical methods · Gilbert Maury

Where should you invest? Follow the evidence.

I start by setting a sustainable cost per acquisition. Then I measure how much demand the campaign created and identify the channel, product and message worth testing next.

Customer value

What CPA can a cohort recover in six months?

Data and scope

I link each customer to their first order and group customers by acquisition month. I track refunds, variable costs and repeat purchases across the entire cohort, including customers who never return.

Cumulative margin per customer at month 6 = total cohort contribution margin at month 6 ÷ initial customer count.

How I make the decision

I compare cohorts at the same age. The first month in which cumulative margin covers CPA gives the payback period. If cash flow requires payback within three months, I set the CPA ceiling at the margin earned by month 3.

Illustrative example: with €47 in margin by month 3 and €63 by month 6, a €50 CPA is covered by six months. It exceeds the three-month ceiling by €3.

Brand awareness

How much additional demand did the campaign create?

Data and scope

I compare on-target reach, frequency and media cost with branded searches, qualified visits and first orders. I check pre-campaign trends, promotions and stockouts.

Cost per incremental new customer = additional spend ÷ estimated additional new customers.

How I make the decision

Where volumes allow, I compare exposed regions with comparable control regions and allow for the time it takes customers to respond. I report the estimated effect and its uncertainty before recommending an increase in reach or frequency.

If branded searches rise but sales are too noisy to interpret, I document the demand signal and extend the test before estimating a return.

Acquisition and activation

Which offer brings customers in, and which brings them back?

Data and scope

I distinguish first orders, repeat purchases and reactivation after a period of inactivity agreed with the CRM team. For each entry product or service, I measure initial margin, time to second purchase and cumulative contribution over the same period.

90-day second-purchase rate = first-time buyers who bought again within 90 days ÷ first-time buyers observed for 90 days.

How I make the decision

I use offers that attract customers with lasting value for prospecting. For offers bought mainly by existing customers, I test a CRM campaign against a control group.

An introductory product may earn little margin on the first order. Its value then depends on observed repeat purchases and what it costs to generate them.

Marginal cost and ROAS

What return does the next budget increase generate?

Data and scope

I use a comparable spending level or a budget test as a baseline. Once the conversion window has elapsed, I estimate incremental customers and revenue, then apply the contribution margin for the resulting sales.

Marginal CPA = Δ spend ÷ Δ new customers. Marginal ROAS = Δ revenue ÷ Δ spend.

How I make the decision

I calculate additional margin after media costs. If spend rises but the extra volume is too small to distinguish from noise, I report a plausible range and adjust the size of the test.

Illustrative example: €1,000 of additional media spend generates €2,200 in incremental revenue. With a 40% margin before media costs, the contribution after media costs is −€120.

Budget forecasting by market

Which budget makes sense for next quarter?

The baseline keeps current spending and channel allocations in place. I compare it with a budget increase or a shift between channels. You see how much extra revenue each option needs to earn its keep and which assumptions would change the decision.

From spend to sales

I fit a power curve relating total spend to market traffic, reviewing unusual periods before excluding any. At channel level, I use ROAS for sales attributed to paid media, cost per visit for consideration campaigns and revenue per session for unpaid traffic. I cross-check channel estimates against total market sales to avoid counting the same order twice.

Putting the forecast to work

Suppose the forecast favours increasing Shopping spend. I check which products bring in first-time buyers, how many units are available and what CPA those customers’ cumulative margin can cover. If the main entry product is nearly sold out, the increase can wait until it is back in stock. Once the plan is live, I compare actuals with the forecast to see where the gap arose: traffic volume, channel mix or revenue per visit.

I tested this approach across six markets using 24 months of data. Estimates were generally close to observed results. Larger gaps appeared in some markets where brand awareness activity seemed more effective. I treat that as a hypothesis to test alongside promotions, launches and stock availability.

Channel allocation

Where should the next euro go across Search, Shopping and Social?

Data and scope

I compare channels using the same margin definition, markets and new-customer criteria. I account for branded search capturing existing demand and the lag between social exposure and an order.

Expected gain from reallocation = additional margin in the channel receiving funds − margin lost in the channel being cut, with total spend held constant.

How I make the decision

I simulate small transfers around observed spending levels. For the chosen scenario, I specify the amount, market, duration and the condition for reverting to the previous budget.

Platform-reported ROAS helps identify opportunities. I evaluate the allocation using deduplicated orders and total margin.

Creative and audience segments

Did the ad perform better because of its message or the audience it reached?

Data and scope

I map each asset to its creative angle, audience segment, placement and frequency. I then break performance down into delivery cost, qualified visits, conversion and net order value.

On a consistent basis: revenue per euro of media spend = qualified visits per euro × orders per qualified visit × net revenue per order.

How I make the decision

I test one hypothesis at a time with comparable audiences and a full conversion window. I then validate performance at higher volume before rolling the creative out more widely.

A video can lower the cost per visit while attracting fewer buyers. This breakdown shows whether the next test should focus on the hook, the offer or the landing page.

Data quality

Does the measurement gap change the decision?

Data and scope

I match order IDs across the order management system, analytics and CRM. I check time zones, currencies, cancellations, returns and tax treatment before attributing a discrepancy to tracking.

Measurement coverage = eligible orders found in both sources ÷ eligible orders in the order management system.

How I make the decision

I break discrepancies down by market, device and payment method. I fix the ones that affect budget decisions first, then set up regular checks on order volume and revenue.

Matching totals can hide equal numbers of duplicate and missing orders. Matching by order ID reveals both.
